        # Modern computer hardware is built from several important components.
Each of them plays a specific role, and together they form a reliable system.
Without proper balance, even expensive parts may not deliver good performance.

        # What does the CPU actually do?
The central processing unit (CPU) is responsible for executing instructions and calculations.
Typical clock speeds range from 3.0GHz to 4.8GHz, depending on the model and workload.
A faster CPU can handle complex tasks more efficiently, but it also requires good cooling.

        # Memory is another critical element. Random access memory (RAM), such as 8GB or 16GB DDR4,
allows applications to run smoothly. Without enough RAM, the system may feel slow,
even when using a powerful processor.

        # Why does storage matter?
Solid-state drives (SSD) with capacities like 512GB or 1TB significantly reduce loading times.
Compared to traditional hard drives, SSDs are quieter, faster, and more reliable.

        # Graphics performance should not be ignored. A graphics card (GPU) with 6GB or 12GB of VRAM
is essential for gaming, video editing, and 3D rendering. For basic office tasks,
however, integrated graphics may be sufficient.

        # Finally, all components rely on a stable power supply. A PSU rated at 550W–750W ensures
safe operation and protects the system from voltage issues. Everything is installed on
a motherboard and enclosed in a case costing around $90–$150, where airflow and cable
management matter more than appearance.
